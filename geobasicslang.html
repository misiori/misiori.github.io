Learn Languages

Recognize the most common scripts and languages on signs.

1

Thai vs. Hindi

Thai is highly curved with loops at the end of lines, often looking like continuous, flowing ribbons. Hindi (Devanagari script) features a distinctive horizontal line running across the top of the letters, with characters hanging below it.

Thai สวัสดี (Hello)
Hindi नमस्ते (Hello)
Thai is used in: Thailand
Hindi is used in: India (especially North/Central), Nepal (partially)
2

Filipino vs. Indonesian

Both use the Latin alphabet, but look at the words. Filipino (Tagalog) uses lots of repeating syllables (e.g., "ng", "ka", "ma") and often lacks the letter "C". Indonesian uses many "ng" and "ny" combinations but frequently uses prefixes like "ber-", "me-", and "ke-".

Filipino Magandang umaga (Good morning)
Indonesian Selamat pagi (Good morning)
Filipino is used in: Philippines
Indonesian is used in: Indonesia
3

Spanish vs. Portuguese

They look similar, but Portuguese uses many diacritics (ã, õ, ç) and often ends words with "-ão". Spanish relies heavily on the letter "ñ" and often ends words with "-ción" or "-dad".

Spanish ¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás? (Hello! How are you?)
Portuguese Olá! Como está? (Hello! How are you?)
Spanish is used in: Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Uruguay, etc.
Portuguese is used in: Brazil, Portugal
4

European Languages

This is tricky, so look for specific letter combinations:

  • Hungarian: Uses "ő" and "ű", and has many "sz" and "gy" combos.
  • Czech: Uses "ě", "š", "č", "ř" (unique to Czech).
  • Polish: Uses "ł", "ą", "ę", "ś", "ż". Look for lots of "cz" and "sz".
  • French: Uses "é", "è", "ê", "ç", "à", "ù". Look for the letter "y" used as a word.
  • German: Uses "ß" (eszett), "ä", "ö", "ü". Look for long compound words.
  • Dutch: Uses "ij" (which looks like a single letter), and "aa", "ee", "oo", "uu" double vowels.
Hungarian Köszönöm (Thank you)
Czech Děkuji (Thank you)
Polish Dziękuję (Thank you)
French Merci (Thank you)
German Danke schön (Thank you)
Dutch Dank je wel (Thank you)
Hungarian: Hungary
Czech: Czechia
Polish: Poland
French: France, Belgium, Switzerland, Canada, Senegal, Ivory Coast, etc.
German: Germany, Austria, Switzerland
Dutch: Netherlands, Belgium (Flanders)

Languages are a hint, not proof

Many countries are multilingual, and border towns often mix languages. A sign in French doesn't always mean France (it could be Belgium, Switzerland, or Canada). Always combine languages with driving side, landscape, and road lines before you guess.
